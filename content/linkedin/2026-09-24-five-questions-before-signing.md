Signing a software quote you don't fully understand is an expensive way to learn procurement.

Here are five questions that take ten minutes to ask and have saved people I know six figures.

1. Is the quote itemized? If you can't see what platform, features, design, and timeline each contribute, you're not holding an estimate. You're holding a guess with a total.

2. What's NOT included? Migration, load testing, app store fees, post-launch support. This is where budgets quietly die, and it's always written somewhere small, or nowhere.

3. What is it assuming? Every estimate carries beliefs: that your existing API is usable, that you'll supply content, that third-party services cost what they cost today. Get the beliefs on paper.

4. What happens when scope changes? Not if. When. Ask about the hourly rate, the change order process, and how revisions are counted. Teams that squirm here will squirm later too.

5. Who actually does the work? The seniors in the pitch meeting and the juniors on the project are different bills. Ask for names and roles, not logos.

None of this is sophisticated. That's the point. Most bad projects were signed by smart people who didn't ask boring questions.

Save this for the next time a proposal lands in your inbox.
